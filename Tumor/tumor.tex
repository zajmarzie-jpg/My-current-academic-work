\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

Sun et al.~\cite{sun2024spatiotemporal} investigated a cancer growth model
incorporating a strong Allee effect, tumor--immune interactions,
and self- and cross-diffusion, with particular emphasis on its
discrete space--time dynamics. We consider the spatially
homogeneous continuous-time kinetics underlying their model.
Writing
\[
r=r_1,\qquad b=b_1,\qquad k=c_2,\qquad
c=c_1,\qquad d=d_1
\]
in the notation of~\cite{sun2024spatiotemporal}, these kinetics take the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tumor-original}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dT}{dt}
&=rT(1-bT)(T-m)-kTI,\\
\frac{dI}{dt}
&=\frac{\rho TI}{\alpha+T}-cTI-dI.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $T$ and $I$ denote the tumor and immune cell populations,
respectively, and $t$ denotes physical time. Throughout,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tumor-parameters}
r,b,k,\rho,\alpha,c,d>0,
\qquad 0<m<\frac{1}{b}.
\end{equation}

The coefficient $r$ sets the scale of tumor growth,
$b^{-1}$ is the tumor carrying capacity, and $m$ is the
strong Allee threshold. The parameter $k$ measures
immune-mediated tumor killing. In the immune equation,
$\rho$ is the maximal per-capita stimulation rate and
$\alpha$ is the tumor population at half-maximal stimulation.
The coefficients $c$ and $d$ describe tumor-induced immune
inactivation and natural immune loss, respectively.
No external immune-cell influx is included.

The biological meaning of the Allee assumption follows from
the tumor per-capita growth function
\[
g(T)=r(1-bT)(T-m).
\]
In the absence of immune cells, this function is negative for
$0<T<m$, positive for $m<T<b^{-1}$, and negative for
$T>b^{-1}$. Thus, the model assumes that sufficiently small
tumor populations cannot sustain positive net growth.
This is a phenomenological assumption about the tumor
population under consideration, rather than a universal
property of cancer growth. Moreover, $m$ is the threshold
for the immune-free subsystem; in the coupled system,
tumor decline is determined by $g(T)-kI$.

The immune per-capita growth function is
\[
h(T)=\frac{\rho T}{\alpha+T}-cT-d.
\]
Its stimulation component increases with tumor abundance
and saturates, whereas the inactivation component grows
linearly. Consequently, increased tumor abundance need not
produce increased net immune growth. The variable $I$
represents the immune population described by these
assumptions, rather than the entire immune system:
when $T=0$, it satisfies $dI/dt=-dI$.

To obtain a polynomial vector field, introduce the time
variable $\tau$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tumor-time}
\frac{dt}{d\tau}=\alpha+T.
\end{equation}
The chain rule transforms~\eqref{eq:tumor-original} into
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tumor-polynomial}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
T'
&=T(\alpha+T)
  \left[r(1-bT)(T-m)-kI\right],\\
I'
&=I\left[-cT^2+(\rho-c\alpha-d)T-d\alpha\right],
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
where prime denotes differentiation with respect to $\tau$.
System~\eqref{eq:tumor-polynomial} is an autonomous planar
polynomial system of degree four. This transformation is
exact and involves no approximation of the saturating
response.

The biologically relevant phase space is
\[
\mathcal Q=\{(T,I)\in\mathbb R^2:T\geq0,\ I\geq0\}.
\]
Both coordinate axes are invariant, and uniqueness of
solutions implies that $\mathcal Q$ is positively invariant.
Since $\alpha+T>0$ on $\mathcal Q$, the original and
polynomial systems are orbitally equivalent there, with
the same time orientation. In particular, their equilibria,
local stability types, periodic orbits, separatrices, and
basins of attraction coincide.

The correspondence also preserves the forward asymptotic
interpretation of trajectories. Indeed, the tumor equation
gives
\[
0\leq T\leq M,\qquad
M=\max\{T(0),b^{-1}\},
\]
because the tumor component cannot increase above its
carrying capacity. Hence, along each forward trajectory,
\[
\alpha\leq\frac{dt}{d\tau}\leq\alpha+M.
\]
The immune component has at most exponential growth on
finite time intervals, so both formulations are forward
complete, and $t\to\infty$ if and only if $\tau\to\infty$.

The rescaled time $\tau$ is an analytical variable.
Physical time is recovered through
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tumor-time-recovery}
t(\tau)-t(0)
=\int_0^\tau \bigl[\alpha+T(s)\bigr]\,ds.
\end{equation}
Therefore, quantitative statements about growth rates,
transient durations, or oscillation periods must be
expressed in the original time variable.

We study~\eqref{eq:tumor-polynomial} as a polynomial
representation of the established tumor--immune kinetics.
Its higher-degree terms arise from the common time
rescaling and do not represent additional biological
interactions. This formulation permits the use of
polynomial dynamical-systems methods while retaining the
biological interpretation of trajectories in $\mathcal Q$.
The equivalence asserted here is restricted to this domain;
an extension of the polynomial vector field outside
$\mathcal Q$ requires separate mathematical interpretation.
